# Where to publish, and what to post

Written 2 November 2026. Nothing here has been posted for you. Each site below needs your own account, and I have not checked that any outside site accepts articles. Their rules change, so read each site's page first.

**The one rule that matters:** every post links back to the matching guide on remarkablecv.com. Google counts links from real sites that people use. Do not buy links or post the same text everywhere, because Google can penalise that.

## Tier 1: free, and you control them (start this week)

1. **LinkedIn**, your own profile and a Remarkable CV company page. Post a short version of one guide a week, with the link in the first comment or at the end. LinkedIn's audience is people who care about careers.
2. **Facebook page and Ghana job-seeker groups.** Join groups where people ask for CV help. Answer real questions first, and share a guide only where the group's rules allow links.
3. **WhatsApp Channel and Status, and a Telegram channel.** These are where Ghanaian job seekers already are. One tip a day, with the guide link now and then.
4. **TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.** Thirty-second tips such as "3 CV mistakes", with the site in your bio. You can film and edit these yourself.
5. **Quora.** Search "how to write a CV in Ghana" and similar. Write a real answer and link the guide where it truly helps.
6. **Medium.** Publish a shorter, reworded version of a guide. In the story's advanced settings, look for the option to set the original link (canonical link) to your guide, so Google knows the guide is the original.
7. **Pinterest.** Make simple pins ("CV mistakes to avoid") that link to the guides. It is free.

## Tier 2: outreach (not guaranteed, so ask politely)

- **Ghanaian job sites with a career-advice section.** For example Jobberman Ghana and MyJobMag Ghana. Look for a "contribute" or "write for us" page, or email them offering a free article written by an HR professional.
- **University career and placement offices and student unions.** Offer a free CV workshop or a resource link. Schools often link out to useful pages.
- **National service and graduate groups** on WhatsApp and Facebook.
- **News and lifestyle sites** (for example MyJoyOnline, GhanaWeb, Graphic Online). Pitch a short feature: "Ghanaian HR professional's CV tips for job seekers". Your story as the founder is a good angle.
- **Radio morning shows and podcasts** with career segments.

## Tier 3: tool directories (free, small value)

A few listings for the product, such as Product Hunt, AlternativeTo and AI-tool directories. Pick two or three. They bring some visitors and links. Skip any that charge for a link.

## A simple four-week plan

- **Week 1:** set up LinkedIn page, Facebook page, WhatsApp Channel. Post "How to write a CV in Ghana" everywhere, in your own words each time.
- **Week 2:** post the cover letter sample. Answer five Quora questions. Make three short videos.
- **Week 3:** post the national service guide to graduate groups. Email five career offices and two job sites.
- **Week 4:** post the nurses, teachers and banking guides to the matching groups. Check Search Console to see which guides get seen.

Write it down: date, platform, link. In Google Search Console, check "Performance" once a week to see which guides get impressions. More posts like the ones that do well.
